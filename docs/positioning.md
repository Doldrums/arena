# Empira: studying recursive self-improvement

Empira connects scientific tasks, research systems and experiment records to study recursive self-improvement (RSI). The question is whether revisions to search, memory, tools or code improve a system's ability to produce subsequent improvements. Comparisons concern the research procedure and its later use, alongside the solutions it produces.

Arena owns tasks, environment and evaluation contracts, registration, records and comparison. Frameworks retain their own search, learning and state. The site addresses researchers choosing tasks, inspecting methods and preparing experiments; the copy should name these operations directly.

## Connection to the Era of Experience

Silver and Sutton describe continual learning through interaction with an environment, with observations and consequences informing future behavior. They include adaptation through context as well as neural-network weight updates. This supplies a broader motivation for studying systems that revise their methods through experience. [Welcome to the Era of Experience](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf).

Learning from experience is broader than RSI. Empira asks whether experience changes the research procedure and whether that change supports further improvement across system revisions. This is our experimental framing, not an RSI result attributed to Silver and Sutton. On the landing page, the short quotation and its page reference introduce this question within the main text.

## Research library and execution registrations

The Participants view includes a research library and pinned framework registrations. Literature entries describe a paper's mechanism and link to its original sources; they may cover solution search, prompt optimization, reward design or changes to research systems. Inclusion does not label every work as demonstrated RSI or as executable in Arena.

A pinned registration identifies a specific framework revision. Adapter availability and supported execution capabilities are separate fields. Paper links, implementation links, project pages and released artifacts retain their different roles.

RE-Bench already supplies open-ended ML research engineering environments and a common task format. [RE-Bench](https://github.com/METR/RE-Bench). ScienceAgentBench evaluates scientific programs, execution results and costs. [ScienceAgentBench](https://github.com/OSU-NLP-Group/ScienceAgentBench). Empira's emphasis is the study of changing research methods through versioned tasks and subsequent experiments; these comparisons do not establish uniqueness.
